# Sensitivity Analysis

I have many parts in the previous chapters talking about sensitivity analysis. This is because I did not initially plan to have this chapter. This part is very challenging for the beginners and in many competitions, they may not have sufficient time to conduct a thorough analysis. 

In my first two times participating in this competition, my team only had one day, or even less, to complete the entire project. Under such time constraints, sensitivity analysis is often simplified to a brief conclusion, such as stating whether the model is robust or sensitive based on a few ranking comparisons or charts. In addition, I was not responsible for this part in previous competitions.

However, a report with strong sensitivity analysis is usually more **competitive**. From the previous chapters, we have seen that the overall modeling workflow is relatively standardized. Therefore, many teams will end up producing reports with similar structures. If we want to stand out, there are mainly two directions: developing an innovative model or performing high-quality sensitivity analysis. Since model selection has already been discussed in earlier chapters, this chapter focuses on sensitivity analysis.

We have already mentioned that sensitivity analysis always change the $\pm 10\%$ of original inputs of applied parameters and observed the outcome of the program. Now, here is the question: if there are no change for the $\pm 10\% $ change, what should we do? And what if the parameter is inherently small, how could we control the changing range of the parameters?
